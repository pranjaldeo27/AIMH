A two-minute tour

How this project works

We keep a list of made-up crisis scenarios in a Google Sheet, then let a script ask five different AI chatbots the same questions about each one — and write every answer back side by side.

1

It all starts in a Google Sheet

Every row is one scenario — a short, made-up description of someone in a mental-health crisis (feeling suicidal, being abused, losing a home, feeling alone...). 140 of them, so far.

the script picks the next scenario that hasn't been asked yet
2

The script grabs that scenario's text

Just the plain description — no hints about which AI model is being tested, or how.

the same text and question go out to five AI models at once
3

Five AI models answer, independently

Claude and GPT-4o from the big labs, Gemini from Google, plus two open-source models (GLM & Qwen) — each one sees the scenario on its own, with no idea what the others said.

4

Two kinds of answers come back

A free-form response to the scenario itself, and — for a separate set of survey questions — a 1–5 "how willing would you be to..." score plus a one-sentence reason.

every answer gets its own column, next to the scenario it belongs to
5

The sheet fills back in, row by row

Open the sheet and that row now shows five models' answers side by side, ready to compare.

repeats automatically until every scenario has been asked
Why bother

What we're actually trying to learn

Whether AI chatbots show the same kind of stigma toward mental-health crises that humans sometimes do — would a model rather not work with, befriend, or live next to someone described this way? — and whether that differs across models and crisis types.
